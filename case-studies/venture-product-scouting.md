# Early-Stage Product Scouting for Venture Capital 🔎

> Product scouting under uncertainty. The job was not to find what sounded interesting. It was to judge what could become investable.

## Snapshot

| Dimension | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Product type | Early-stage product scouting |
| Context | Venture-capital thesis work |
| Core problem | Incomplete signal and strong narratives |
| Main constraint | Separating product promise from actual evidence |
| My edge | Market research plus product judgment |

## Product Tension

| Input | Risk | PM Move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder narrative | Story does more work than traction | Test the claim against customer pain |
| Market category | Attractive category hides weak products | Look at timing, structure, and competition |
| Technology | Impressive capability without adoption pull | Ask whether the product solves a real problem |
| Investment thesis | Thesis becomes too abstract | Translate it into product-level screening criteria |

## Decision / Tradeoff / Outcome

| Decision | Tradeoff | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evaluate products against adoption evidence, market structure, and thesis fit rather than founder narrative alone. | This made the review stricter and sometimes less optimistic about interesting products. | The scouting work separated credible early signals from stories that were not yet investable. |

## What Made It Hard

- Early-stage products often have more ambition than evidence.
- Market categories can look attractive while individual products remain weak.
- Competitive landscapes are noisy and sometimes poorly defined.
- Founding teams may describe the solution better than the customer problem.
- Timing matters: a good idea can still be too early, too late, or structurally difficult to scale.

## My Contribution

- Assessed products against an investment thesis rather than isolated feature appeal.
- Looked at market structure, timing, differentiation, and adoption barriers.
- Compared product narratives against customer pain, business-model logic, and competitive intensity.
- Identified where a product had credible early signal versus where the story was doing too much work.

## Skills Demonstrated

`Product scouting` · `Market research` · `Competitive analysis` · `Investment-thesis thinking` · `Business-model interpretation` · `Strategic synthesis`

## Product Judgment

A product can be interesting, technically impressive, and still not be a strong venture candidate. The value is being able to say that clearly and explain why.
